Elevator design

This past weekend, my boyfriend David posed the following question: how would you design an algorithm for an elevator?

David astutely observed that with the way elevators are designed, we miss a critical piece of information: how many people are waiting to be picked up by the elevator at a given floor, or how many people inside the elevator would like to go to a certain floor. We just know that if a floor is selected, at least one person would like to go there. This is a hinderance in designing an algorithm that would focus on minimizing people's wait times.

David also pointed out the tradeoff between fairness and global efficiency. A fair algorithm would prioritize people based on when they requested the elevator - first come, first served. On the other hand, one could design an algorithm that aimed to minimize the cumulative wait time of people.

The mechanics of the elevator matter too. Say the elevator is fast. Then stopping at a floor becomes relatively more costly.

Let's build up a solution gradually. To start - what real-time information are we getting? There's two types. First, there's requests: people requesting the elevator to come to their floor, and the didrection they intend to go to. Let's denote a request by \((f_i, s_i, t_i)\), where \(f_i\) denotes the floor number, \(s_i\in \{-1, 1\}\) denotes the direction, and \(t_i\) denotes the time of the request. Second, there's people who have just gotten in the elevator, and indicate the floor they would like to be taken to. We'll denote these requests by \((d_i, t_i)\) where \(d_i\) denotes the destination floor.

Let's try to recreate the algorithm that most elevators use. Here's my guess: let's say the elevator is currently going in a direction (either up or down). Then, it will keep a list of priority and target floors above and below it at all times. Target floors are those where: someone in the elevator would like to be dropped off. Priority floors are those where someone is requesting to be picked up at, moving in the same direction. Once the elevator has no target floors in that direction, it chooses a new direction based on who is the earliest priority floor.

What are some inefficiencies of this algorithm? First, where the elevator "parks" is important. If most people enter or exit the elevator at the first floor, parking these might make sense. This is not a behavior I observe in some elevators.
